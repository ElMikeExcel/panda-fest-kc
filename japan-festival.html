KC Japan Festival 2026

A detailed guide to the food, sumo, activities, shopping and kid-friendly experiences.

The pitch: This is the do-stuff festival: Japanese food is part of it, but the reason to spend the day here is sumo, taiko, anime, martial arts, crafts, shopping, workshops and cultural demonstrations.

🥋 What you're actually paying to see

🤼 Sumo

Former professional Japanese sumo wrestler Tatsuosho and the Shōgeki Sumo Team, with demonstrations, taiko and audience participation. There is also a meet-and-greet.

🥁 Taiko

Live Japanese drumming plus opportunities/workshops to experience taiko rather than only watching it.

⚔️ Martial arts

Kendo, karate, kenjutsu, naginata and tessenjutsu/war-fan demonstrations are among the listed programming.

👘 Traditional culture

Kimono/yukata dressing, tea ceremony, samurai armor, bonsai, ikebana, origami, calligraphy and Japanese garden programming.

🎮 Anime + games

Anime screenings, manga/anime areas, cosplay, a video-game room and Japanese pop-culture vendors.

🎵 Performance

Koto, shamisen, shakuhachi, traditional dance, J-Pop/Studio Ghibli music and Japanese comedy/storytelling are represented on the schedule.

👧 Strong kid stuff

The festival has a Japanese Kids Cultural Village, traditional outdoor children's games with prizes, a children's mikoshi parade, kids' Japanese-language activities, origami, make-your-own koinobori and a Sumo Stamp Passport scavenger activity.

🛍 Shopping

There are 30+ listed booths across the campus with anime merchandise, gachapon, Japanese toys, art, clothing, books and crafts. The Japanese Bazaar adds kimono, yukata, happi coats, kokeshi dolls, games, ceramics, serving ware, sake sets and tea sets.

🗓 Family game plan

Don't treat this like a food fair. Pick 3–5 scheduled things you want to see, then fit food/shopping between them. Sumo is the obvious anchor attraction for 2026. Arriving early gives you the best chance to understand the campus layout before bouncing between buildings.

🔎 Know before you go

Is it mainly a food festival?No. Food is a meaningful part of the day, but the unusually large scheduled cultural/activity program is the main differentiator.
Is it redundant if we're also doing Panda Fest?No. Panda Fest is much more food/vendor driven; Japan Festival is much more schedule/activity/culture driven.
